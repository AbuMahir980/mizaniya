Options A and B combined — Jamiu's decision, 25 September. There is no gauge at this width. Not because it is a poor chart, though it is: gauges waste space, carry no context, and compare badly, because people do not read angles as quickly or as accurately as they read lengths against a common baseline. It went because it answers a question nobody asks at a desk. Can I spend this, right now? is asked standing in a shop with a phone in your hand, and a dial is a good answer to it. At a laptop, eleven days into a cycle, the question is am I on pace, and what is pulling me off it? — and a dial has no memory of yesterday and no opinion about tomorrow. The gauge stays at 360, where it is right.

Two columns, full height, and that is the whole layout rule. Left is the answer — what is safe today, how that stands against the plan, and where the money is. Right is the reason — every category against its own pace. Read left to right and you get the figure, then why it is that figure.

The first build had a hole in it. A wide pace chart across the top pushed everything else down, leaving a band of empty paper under the left column and nineteen empty days on the right. So the chart is no longer a card — it is a sparkline inside the card whose figure it explains, at the size two lines actually need — and the categories run in one column so both sides end together. Every bar runs 0 to 150% of its own allowance, which puts expected by now at 24.4% and the whole allowance at two-thirds on every row: the ticks line up in columns down the card, and bar length means one thing everywhere.
