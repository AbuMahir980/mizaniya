Why the gauge is going. Not because it is an inefficient chart — though it is: Tableau's case against gauges is that they waste space, carry no context, and compare badly, because humans do not read angles as quickly or as accurately as they read lengths against a common baseline. The real reason is that it answers the wrong question. “Can I spend this, right now?” is asked standing in a shop with a phone in your hand, and a dial is a good answer to it. At a desk, eleven days into a cycle, the question is “am I on pace, and what is pulling me off it?” — and a dial has no memory of yesterday and no opinion about tomorrow. The 1440 screen was answering the 360 screen's question at four times the size. The gauge stays at 360, where it is right.

Eleven days of memory and nineteen days of consequence, in one object. The solid line is what has actually gone; the dashed line is what the plan expected by each day. The gap between them at today is the whole story — ₦14,666.74 ahead of pace — and it is the only figure on this screen that a phone physically cannot show you. Nothing animates on the way to it (motion.md §0): the line is drawn at its final length on first paint. Strongest for: the one question a desk is for. Weakest at: it says you are behind without saying which category did it.
