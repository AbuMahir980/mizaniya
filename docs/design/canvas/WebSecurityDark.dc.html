We never store your bank data in readable form.
The question worth asking of any company is not how they store your information — it is what they hold, and why. Here is ours. Every line names a purpose, because a line that cannot name one is a line that should not be here.
What we hold, and what for
Your email address
To sign you in, and to send a reset if you lose your password.
Until you delete the account
Your budget — categories, amounts, movements, debts
It is the product. Without it there is nothing to show you.
Until you delete it
A bank connection, only if you turn one on
So we can see that money moved and ask you which envelope it belongs to.
Until you disconnect it
A list of your signed-in devices
So you can sign a lost phone out from a device you still have.
Until you remove the device
What we do not hold at all
Your card number
Payment goes straight to the processor. We never see it and never store it — not the full number, not the last four digits.
Your phone number, address, date of birth, BVN or NIN
We have no use for any of them, so we do not ask.
Your contacts, your location, or anything for advertising
There is no advertising in Mizaniya and there is nothing to sell.
How we decide
We keep only what we can name a use for
If we cannot say what a piece of information is for, we should not be holding it. That is the test every row above has to pass.
Nobody builds Mizaniya against your figures
Development and testing run on made-up households. Your budget is not a test fixture.
Deleting your account deletes the data
Not just the login. The budget goes with it.
If you connect a bank, our server sees the movement in order to pass it to you.
We will not pretend otherwise. You are deciding whether to trust us with a salary, and a sentence that sounds better than the truth is not worth what it costs. Connecting a bank is optional, it is off until you turn it on, and it is read-only — it can see money move and cannot move money.

A PUBLIC page, in the landing page's frame — not an app screen. The first version was drawn inside the app shell, with a sidebar and an Add button. But whoever clicks Security and privacy in the footer has no account, no budget and no data: dropping them into a dashboard frame with a navigation they cannot use is a screen that does not know who is reading it. Same nav, same footer, one column.

And it no longer explains the database. The first version said keys are held outside the database, that people can read production data, that backups persist 30 days. All architecture — and the owner had already ruled once that you do not tell people how your data is stored. I wrote it anyway, dressed as candour. Telling someone the shape of your storage is not honesty, it is a map.

The frame that replaced it came from the owner: compliance does not ask how you store it, it asks what you hold and why. An auditor points at a customer's phone number and says — what is this for? If you cannot name the purpose you should not be holding it. So the page is a table of what we hold and its reason, a list of what we do not hold at all (no card number, not even the last four; no BVN, no NIN, no phone number), and how we decide. Every row names a purpose, because a row that cannot is a row that should not exist. The three retention facts I invented are gone with the architecture that needed them — item 76 is withdrawn.
